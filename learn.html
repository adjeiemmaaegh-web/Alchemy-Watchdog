MODULE 01 · 5 MIN Not started

Understand Watchdog

Watchdog helps people review potentially relevant activity. It is a development-preview investigation assistant—not a proven shoplifting detector or a tool for automatic accusations.

What you’ll learn

Understand the difference between an observation and a verified incident.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Observe

    The system may surface configured zones, timing and other review candidates.

  2. 2
    Check context

    An authorized reviewer looks at the available supporting information.

  3. 3
    Decide carefully

    Document what is known and what remains uncertain.

✦
Remember

An alert is a prompt to investigate. It is never evidence by itself that someone committed theft.

MODULE 02 · 5 MIN Not started

Open the preview safely

Use only the development build supplied through your approved testing process. There is no verified public Windows installer on this website.

What you’ll learn

Recognize what to do when a screen, file or test build is unexpected.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Verify the source

    Confirm the build and instructions came from your authorized coordinator.

  2. 2
    Open carefully

    Use documented launch steps for that exact preview; don't disable Windows security.

  3. 3
    Report differences

    Take note of unexpected screens or errors and request instructions.

✦
Remember

MODULE 03 · 6 MIN Not started

Create an administrator

The tested Manage Stores console includes first-administrator setup and a separate sign-in flow. Use fictional test identities when learning.

What you’ll learn

Set up access without sharing credentials or exposing real personal data.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Choose a test identity

    Use a fictional operator ID rather than someone's personal information.

  2. 2
    Protect the password

    Create a unique password of at least 12 characters and keep it private.

  3. 3
    Sign in separately

    After first-admin setup, use the sign-in flow to access protected functions.

✦
Remember

Do not photograph, send or publish administrator passwords as part of a test report.

MODULE 04 · 6 MIN Not started

Create stores & permissions

Stores are separate workspaces, and staff must only access the stores for which they are explicitly authorized. Final permission hardening is still under test.

What you’ll learn

Understand store separation and the principle of least privilege.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Add a test store

    Enter a fictional Store ID and Display Name using the documented preview.

  2. 2
    Check the result

    Use List stores to confirm that the intended test store exists.

  3. 3
    Grant limited access

    Use separate operator accounts and authorized assignments; avoid shared admin login.

✦
Remember

The store-access illustration is conceptual. Do not treat it as an exact representation of the present management UI.

MODULE 05 · 7 MIN Not started

Understand cameras & AI

Capture rate, processing rate and AI results are different things. CPU-only local inference has been demonstrated with synthetic test examples, but real CCTV understanding and field accuracy are unverified.

What you’ll learn

Interpret camera output without assuming every frame or event has been analyzed.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Capture with permission

    Only use camera feeds and footage you are authorized to view.

  2. 2
    Check processing limits

    A camera can capture frames that the application does not analyze.

  3. 3
    Require human judgment

    Treat generated descriptions as unverified until supported by actual evidence.

✦
Remember

CPU-friendly design is a performance goal, not evidence that the system can identify theft or a person's intent.

MODULE 06 · 7 MIN Not started

Review incidents responsibly

A responsible incident review separates observed facts, available evidence and uncertain interpretations. Review functions are restricted to authorized users.

What you’ll learn

Create a clear, fair review note that avoids ungrounded allegations.

Your learning steps

  1. 1
    Record the observation

    Note only what is visible, with approximate time and source.

  2. 2
    Check the evidence

    Inspect relevant authorized media and any contextual records.

  3. 3
    Document a careful outcome

    Record the review steps, unresolved questions and who approved the decision.

✦
Remember

Never infer identity, intent or guilt from proximity, appearance or an automated confidence score.
